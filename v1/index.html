How to read this model

This is an exploratory reconstruction of your Antarkya description. Numerical assumptions complete the unfinished rules. Results show the consequences of these rules; they are not estimates for a real society.

The red/green state measures an agent’s balance of corrupt and honest decisions. It does not directly measure an attitude of tolerance. Citizens who pay under extortion are counted as victims, separately from voluntary corrupt exchanges.

Rules and implementation assumptions

From the description. Four agent types start green. Objective and subjective preferences remain fixed. Citizen–business trade is honest and leaves scores and reputation unchanged. Other cross-role encounters may involve bribery, vote-buying or political influence. Networks and memory can reinforce corruption. Elections remove a politician only when both thresholds are crossed.

Decisions. Decision score = weighted objective gain + weighted subjective gain + preference adjustment + memory and network benefits − weighted punishment risk − weighted subjective cost. Corruption requires a score greater than the decision threshold (default 0.5) for both participants. A completed voluntary exchange adds 1 to each score; a declined exchange subtracts 1 from each. Red means score / decision count exceeds 0.2. Neutral trade is excluded from that denominator.

Numerical assumptions. Objective gain is uniform from 0.5 to 1.5, multiplied by the economy coefficient (low 0.8, medium 0.65, high 0.5). Subjective gain equals the economy’s social coefficient (0.2, 0.45, 0.7) × (2 × partner reputation − 1). Higher subjective weights therefore favor honesty around honest partners. Preference contributes +0.08 for objective or −0.08 for subjective. Memory adds 0.25 × partner reputation; a repeated tie adds 0.15. Both influences are adjustable.

Weights and exposure. Citizens and officials draw subjective benefit/cost weights from 0.6–1 and objective weights from 0–0.4; businesses and politicians reverse these ranges. Objective preference probability is 20% or 80%, respectively. Weights are fixed for an agent’s lifetime. Weak/medium/strong law gives punishment probabilities 0.2/0.45/0.7. Unequal exposure multiplies these by 1 for citizens, 0.65 for businesses, 0.8 for officials and 0.25 for politicians. Subjective cost uses the mean of economic stigma (0.3/0.4/0.5) and legal stigma (0.3/0.4/0.5).

Memory and networks. Reputation is the share of remembered eligible encounters that were corrupt. Unknown partners have reputation 0.75 if red, 0.1 otherwise. Three completed corrupt exchanges establish a repeated tie; it expires after 50 rounds without renewal. Agents move randomly with a small pull toward network partners. Contact attempts sample neighbors within the encounter radius; a pair meets at most once per round.

Extortion and sanctions. A red official can initiate an extortion attempt using a +0.15 decision bonus. A citizen pays if a random refusal cost (0.4–1.2 × economy gain) exceeds 0.35 × their subjective weight plus their expected weighted legal cost. Extortion raises the official’s score, but does not label the victim corrupt. Sanctions after completed corrupt acts make perpetrators inactive for three following rounds. They do not erase scores.

Support and elections. Approval probability = 0.5 + 0.35 × citizen subjective weight − 0.8 × subjective weight × perceived corruption + 0.2 × objective weight × acquired loyalty, with a 0.1 bonus for matching preferences; constrained to 0–1. Approval is independent for each politician, so it need not total 100%. Elections sample each citizen’s approval. A politician is demoted only if score > the corruption threshold AND approval < the support threshold. Between elections the display shows expected approval. No citizens means elections are skipped.

Campaigns and replacement. Campaigns recur before every election and end on election day. Politicians receive two independent 20% chances of a +0.1 decision bonus, plus a 20% chance of an extra encounter attempt. Vote-buying adds 0.25 loyalty, decaying by 3% per round. Demotion changes the role to citizen and resets score and decision count; lifetime counts and preferences remain. Optional replacement recruits an eligible existing citizen, preserving total population; seats remain vacant if none are eligible.

These choices resolve gaps or inconsistencies in the source description. They are documented in MODEL.md and can be revised as you refine the theory. The model does not include a separate media agent or broadcast system because these were absent from the supplied description.
